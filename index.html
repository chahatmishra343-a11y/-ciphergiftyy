The Weaver of Secrets
I was sixteen when I began collecting things that could not be touched.
Missed conversations. Half-written letters. Unsent confessions. The strange ache of loving someone from a distance.

I noticed that people spend fortunes on flowers that wilt in days, yet the smallest message can remain alive for years.

So I built a garden that does not die. I am not a florist. I do not arrange roses. I arrange feelings.
— The Digital Florist

Once upon a time, people pressed flowers between pages.
Today, we press memories into links.

Every gift here begins as an empty canvas. A bouquet. A letter. A candy jar. A photograph. A memory. You fill it with your own story.

Then it becomes something no one else can recreate. A private little world that belongs to only two people. Not because it is expensive. Because it is personal.
